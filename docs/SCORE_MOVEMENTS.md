# Score movements

The storyline, one movement per chapter. Each movement is a region of Terra, a set of
dungeons in it, a roster of things living in them, and one item that unlocks the next
movement.

This is the *design* document — what the content is and what it is for. For how any of it is
built, see [WORLDGEN.md](WORLDGEN.md) (the map and its biomes), [BOSSES.md](BOSSES.md) (the
boss skeleton), [SPAWNING.md](SPAWNING.md) (making a biome produce a mob), and the main
[README](../README.md) (project layout and the naming conventions).

## Status

**Everything below is scaffolding.** The mobs are placeholder cube models, the dungeons are
generated by a Python script rather than built, and *nothing in the mod spawns naturally* —
every mob is reached through its spawn egg or placed by a structure. They exist so a movement
can be walked end to end and its pacing judged, which is the only question a placeholder
answers.

| Movement | Code | Region | Dungeons | Mobs | Ends with |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| I — Columbia: Those who Take the Future | `dv_`, `mb_` | Columbia | 3 | 8 | Blueprint: Originium Refinement |
| II — Ægir: Glimpse of the Depths | `sv_` | Ægir / Iberian coast | 1 | 7 | *not decided* |

### Outside the movements

**Originium Slug** — 8 HP, 2 damage, speed 0.32. Wasteland vermin, belonging to no dungeon
and carrying no code. It used to eat the charge out of any Forge-Energy block *and* burst
corrosively on death; both were cut, along with the `Machines` helper that found the machines
and its natural spawn. What is left is a plain melee mob with nothing on it — deliberately, so
that whatever replaces those mechanics hangs off an empty thing rather than a half-removed
one.

## Movement I — Columbia: Those who Take the Future

Land in Columbia, survive the wastes, clear three dungeons, come away with the recipe for
refining raw Originium safely — and with it the road out. Spawn is in Columbia because
`ORIGIN_AT_BLOCK` in `TerraMap` puts it there, so this is the movement the player is standing
in before they have chosen anything.

### Mansfield Break — `mb_`

A prison, and the projection of a version of events where the break succeeded.

| Mob | Role |
|---|---|
| **Imprisoned Pugilist** | 22 HP, 4 damage. A zombie by another name — the body that fills a corridor. |
| **Imprisoned Recidivist** | 45 HP, 8 damage, 0.5 knockback resistance. The Pugilist half again as large and too heavy to walk backwards, so a corridor with one in it is fought through rather than kited out of. |
| **Imprisoned Sniper** | 20 HP. Fires real vanilla arrows on `RangedBowAttackGoal` — strafes, backs off, holds fire without line of sight. |
| **Jesselton Williams** — *boss* | 220 HP, two phases. Drops the **Mansfield Master Key**. |

Jesselton is not this world's Jesselton: the effigy in his cell block reaches *sideways*
rather than backwards, and what comes through is the mercenary who won, wearing a confidence
the player never got. Phase one is heavy kinetic Arts that the prison's own riot gear
answers; below half health he switches to `priestess:void_arts`, which is in
`bypasses_armor`, and the gear stops helping. The player does not see a new attack — they see
the same attack stop being something armour answers.

### Dorothy's Vision — `dv_`

| Mob | Role |
|---|---|
| **Failure** | 24 HP, no armour. The attempt that did not take, still walking. Drops **Medium**. |
| **Replica** | 34 HP, 2 armour. The attempt that took, and came out wrong in the way a copy does. Drops **Medium**. |
| **Bionic** | 60 HP, 8 armour, 0.6 knockback resistance. Finished with hardware when the flesh ran out. Drops 2 **Medium**. |
| **"Awaken"** — *boss* | 300 HP, immobile, summoned by Dorothy's Terminal. Drops **Dreamland**. |

The three are a ladder rather than a set — the same fight at three weights, which is what
makes Medium feel cheap at the bottom and worth something at the top.

> **"Awaken" has no attacks.** It hovers, watches, and takes damage until it dies. Everything
> needed to give it one is wired — target selectors run, `ArtsBeam` exists — and the gap is
> deliberate and documented in the class. It is the one place in the mod where an immobile
> boss cannot reach the player, which `Sal Viento Bishop Quintus` deliberately does not repeat.

### Rhine Lab

No mobs of its own yet. The Director's Office holds **Blueprint: Originium Refinement** in a
chest — a stand-in for rebooting the Archival Mainframe, and the loot pool to delete when the
mainframe exists.

### What Movement I hands you

| Item | From | Gates |
|---|---|---|
| **Mansfield Master Key** | Jesselton Williams | nothing yet |
| **Dorothy's Neural Processor** | *nothing — its mob was cut* | nothing yet |
| **Medium** | Failure / Replica / Bionic | nothing yet |
| **Dreamland** | "Awaken" | nothing yet |
| **Blueprint: Originium Refinement** | Rhine Lab chest | the end of the movement |

Nothing consumes any of them. They exist, they are named and they are obtainable, which is
what makes the movement something you can walk through rather than something written down.

**Dorothy's Neural Processor is currently unobtainable** — it dropped from the Failed Vision,
which was removed. Either give it to another mob or delete it.

## Movement II — Ægir: Glimpse of the Depths

Ægir is in the ocean south of Iberia, and the two are at war — which is why Iberia's coast is
dead and why the Deep Sea took the warmth with it. This movement is the first look at what is
down there.

### Under Tides — `sv_`

The code is `sv_`, for **Sal Viento**, the coastal town the dungeon is built around. The
package is `mobs/undertides/`; the code and the folder name deliberately do not match — see
[the note in the README](../README.md#the-dungeon-code).

Unlike Mansfield's ladder, the five trash mobs are built as a rock-paper-scissors. No two are
answered the same way, which is the whole design of the movement's combat:

| Mob | HP | Speed | Damage | The problem it poses |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---|
| **Runner** | 18 | **0.34** | 4 | The only mob in the mod faster than a sprinting player. You cannot leave — kill it. |
| **Crawler** | 12 | 0.31 | 3 | 0.7 blocks tall, so it sits *under* a normal swing arc. Clearing them is a choice you make, not something that happens while you fight what is behind them. |
| **Reaper** | 40 | 0.22 | 10 | Hits hard and moves slower than you walk. Everything it does has to be walked into. |
| **Piercer** | 16 | 0.28 | **11** | Highest raw damage in the movement on the frailest body. Notice it first or wear it. |
| **Spitter** | 20 | 0.20 | 5 @ 12 blocks | Outranges everything. Break line of sight. |

| Boss | |
|---|---|
| **The First to Talk** — *miniboss* | 120 HP, melee, boss bar. Enrages at half health: the bar turns red and it speeds up, and it **persists that across a reload** — which is the bug `BOSSES.md` records against Jesselton, fixed here rather than repeated. |
| **Sal Viento Bishop Quintus** — *boss* | 400 HP, **fully immobile**. Fires a hitscan beam every 2.5 s, but only inside a 40° arc it turns toward at 1.8°/tick — so getting behind it is the only thing resembling a dodge, and the turn rate is the difficulty dial. |

### What Movement II hands you

**Nothing yet.** No Ægir mob drops a progression item and there is no equivalent of the
Blueprint, so the movement currently ends when Quintus does and hands the player nothing to
carry forward. That is the largest open question in it.

Two placeholders worth knowing about:

- **The Spitter and Quintus both borrow `priestess:originium_acid`** for their attacks. Wrong
  flavour for anything from under the sea — its death message reads "dissolved in Originium
  acid" — but it already existed, is tagged the way a corrosive cloud should be, and had no
  user at all after the Originium Slug's death burst was cut. An Ægir damage type is what
  both want.
- **The Piercer does not pierce.** It deals raw damage; armour reduces it normally. Making
  the name literal is a damage type in `minecraft:bypasses_armor`, and the number should come
  down when it goes on — 11 armour-piercing would be the hardest hit anywhere in the mod.

## How a movement is wired

Adding one is four things, none of which need new machinery:

1. **A code.** Two letters, lowercase for ids and assets, `Pascal` for classes — see
   [the README](../README.md#the-dungeon-code). It groups the movement in `/summon`
   autocomplete, in the creative tab, in the asset folders and in the class list, which are
   otherwise one alphabetical run.
2. **A package** under `entity/mobs/`, named for the dungeon. Bosses go to `entity/bosses/`
   and minibosses to `entity/minibosses/` regardless of movement — those directories are about
   a mob's *tier*, not its chapter, so they cut across.
3. **Registrations** in `ModEntities`, `ModItems` (spawn eggs), `PriestessClient` (renderers)
   and `ModLanguageProvider` (display names, which never carry the code).
4. **Somewhere to be.** Currently nothing: no mob in the mod spawns naturally, and the two
   structure-placed bosses are the only things not reached by egg. See
   [SPAWNING.md](SPAWNING.md).

## What every movement shares

- **Oripathy** — the invisible per-player infection. Nothing in either movement raises it
  except ambient exposure in `priestess:terra`. See the [README](../README.md#oripathy).
- **`ArtsBeam`** — the hitscan ranged attack. Every ranged attacker except the Imprisoned
  Sniper uses it, which means cover is the counterplay rather than dodging, and it is why the
  dungeons want pillars in them.
- **`BossMonster`** — the bar, and not being something the world can quietly take away.
- **`GeoMonster`** — the melee goal set and the GeckoLib plumbing, shared by every trash mob.
- **Placeholder art** — `tools/generate_placeholder_art.py` for textures,
  `tools/generate_placeholder_models.py` for the `.geo.json`. Both are idempotent and seeded;
  overwrite an output with real art and remove its entry from the script.
